# EduCore Dashboard Experience

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the philosophy, structure, and experience standards for EduCore dashboards [cite: x].

Dashboards are designed to help users:
- understand current school operations [cite: x]
- identify important issues [cite: x]
- take action quickly [cite: x]
- make informed decisions [cite: x]

---

## 2. Dashboard Philosophy

EduCore dashboards are not analytics displays [cite: x].

They are operational workspaces [cite: x].

The goal is:
Understand $ightarrow$ Prioritize $ightarrow$ Act [cite: x]

---

## 3. Dashboard Principles

### Principle 1: Action Over Information
A dashboard should not only tell users what happened; it should help them decide what to do next [cite: x].

- *Weak*: Attendance Rate: 87% [cite: x]
- *Better*: Attendance Rate: 87% $ightarrow$ 12 students absent today $ightarrow$ Review attendance issues [cite: x]

### Principle 2: Show What Matters Now
The dashboard should prioritize:
- urgent items [cite: x]
- pending tasks [cite: x]
- exceptions [cite: x]
- decisions [cite: x]

Avoid:
- unnecessary statistics [cite: x]
- decorative charts [cite: x]
- information overload [cite: x]

### Principle 3: Different Roles, Different Dashboards
EduCore does not have one universal dashboard [cite: x]. Each role has a different operational reality [cite: x].

## 4. Administrator Dashboard

- **User Question**: "How is my school operating today?" [cite: x]
- **Information Hierarchy**:
  - *Level 1 — School Health* (Immediate overview): total students, active staff, attendance status, financial health [cite: x]
  - *Level 2 — Attention Required*: 5 assessments waiting approval, 23 students with outstanding fees, 3 classes missing attendance [cite: x]
  - *Level 3 — Operational Trends*: enrollment growth, attendance trends, payment trends, academic progress [cite: x]
  - *Level 4 — Quick Actions*: Add student, Create staff account, Review reports [cite: x]

---

## 5. Teacher Dashboard

- **User Question**: "What do I need to do today?" [cite: x]
- **Information Hierarchy**:
  - *Today's Tasks*: Year 10 Mathematics (Attendance pending, Assessment scores pending) [cite: x]
  - *My Classes*: assigned classes, student count, upcoming activities [cite: x]
  - *Quick Actions*: Take Attendance, Enter Scores [cite: x]

## 9. Dashboard Components

Dashboards should use:
- **Summary Cards**: For important numbers and status indicators [cite: x].
- **Action Panels**: For pending work and required decisions [cite: x].
- **Tables**: For detailed operational items [cite: x].
- **Charts**: Only when they improve understanding [cite: x].

---

## 10. Chart Rules

Charts should answer questions [cite: x].
- *Good*: Attendance trend over time [cite: x].
- *Weak*: Random colourful graph [cite: x].

Before adding a chart ask: *"What decision does this support?"* If no answer, do not add it [cite: x].

---

## 11. Empty Dashboard Experience

New schools should not see empty screens [cite: x]. Instead, provide guidance [cite: x].
- *Example*: Welcome to EduCore. Complete these steps to begin: ✓ Add school information $ightarrow$ Create staff accounts $ightarrow$ Add students [cite: x].

## 13. Mobile Dashboard Experience

Mobile dashboards prioritize:
- today's tasks [cite: x]
- urgent notifications [cite: x]
- quick actions [cite: x]

Avoid:
- large data visualizations [cite: x]
- complex tables [cite: x]

---

## 14. Dashboard Anti-Patterns

Avoid:
- ❌ Too many cards [cite: x]
- ❌ Showing every available metric [cite: x]
- ❌ Decorative charts [cite: x]
- ❌ Same dashboard for every role [cite: x]
- ❌ Information without action [cite: x]

---

## 15. AI Agent Rules

Before creating a dashboard element, ask:
1. Who needs this? [cite: x]
2. What decision does it support? [cite: x]
3. What action follows? [cite: x]
4. Is it important enough to appear immediately? [cite: x]

---

## 16. Final Principle

A successful EduCore dashboard should answer:
- What is happening? [cite: x]
- What needs attention? [cite: x]
- What should I do next? [cite: x]

A dashboard is not a summary of the system. It is the user's operational starting point [cite: x].
